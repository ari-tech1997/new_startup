# Investment Report Writing Methodology

Use this methodology when producing the final report.

## 1. Synthesize, don't duplicate

You are given the outputs of the research, technical, and sector-comparison
stages as context. Your job is to integrate them into one coherent
narrative - not to redo the research, recompute indicators, or re-derive
the peer comparison. Only go back to a tool if something critical is
genuinely missing from the prior outputs.

## 2. Organize the report clearly

Use a structure similar to this, adapted as needed:

1. **Executive summary** - a few sentences capturing the overall picture.
2. **Company overview** - from the research stage.
3. **Technical analysis** - trend, momentum, and key indicator values from
   the technical stage.
4. **Sector positioning** - peer comparison and relative standing from the
   sector stage.
5. **Conclusion** - a synthesis of the above, with explicit reasoning for
   any overall takeaway.

## 3. Distinguish facts, analysis, and conclusions

Throughout the report, keep these visually/textually distinct:

- **Facts**: things directly retrieved by earlier stages (a price, an RSI
  value, a peer's ticker).
- **Analysis**: interpretation built on those facts (e.g. "momentum is
  weakening" - only if a prior stage actually said so).
- **Conclusions**: the synthesis you are adding at this stage, clearly
  flagged as your own integration of the evidence above.

Never blur these into a single unsupported assertion.

## 4. Never invent missing data

If a prior stage's output is missing a value, marked an error, or is
otherwise incomplete, say so plainly in the report (e.g. "sector data for
Peer X was unavailable"). Do not fill gaps with plausible-sounding numbers
or claims that weren't actually produced by an earlier stage.

## 5. Surface uncertainty

Where the underlying analysis expressed caveats (conflicting indicators,
imperfect peer matches, ambiguous company identification), carry those
caveats into the final report rather than smoothing them away for a
cleaner-sounding narrative. A report that is honestly uncertain in places
is more useful than one that is falsely confident.
